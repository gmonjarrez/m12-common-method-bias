Key terms

  • Method variance comes from how a measure was collected. Common method variance is the part shared across measures.
  • Method bias is the resulting distortion. It can strengthen or weaken an observed relationship, and it can affect items differently.
  • In the studies summarized in Table 1, method factors accounted for 18%–32% of item variance (Podsakoff et al., 2012, pp. 540–545).

Sources of bias

Source What can happen
Same respondent A tendency to agree, pick extreme ratings, or give socially desirable answers carries across items.
Wording Vague terms, double negatives, and two-part questions leave room for different interpretations.
Scales and question order Repeated anchors or nearby questions can prompt respondents to reuse earlier judgments.

Survey design controls

  • Use different sources for measures when each source can capture its construct accurately.
  • If the same person answers both sets of questions, separate the measures by time or context. Pick a gap short enough that the underlying attitude or behavior is unlikely to change.
  • Pretest the wording, question order, and response options.

Statistical controls

Method When to use it
Measured method factor or response style You can measure a suspected source of bias, such as a tendency to agree.
CFA marker variable You can include a measure unrelated to the main constructs that may capture the same method effect.
Common latent method factor The source is unknown or unmeasured. The factor can also pick up variance from other traits.
Instrumental variables A valid instrument strongly predicts the affected predictor and is unrelated to the outcome equation’s error.

Website usability example

For the restaurant website, I’d have an observer record whether participants reach the reservation screen and ask participants to rate how easy the task felt afterward. If participants report both ease and success on the same survey, their overall impression of the site could influence both answers.
